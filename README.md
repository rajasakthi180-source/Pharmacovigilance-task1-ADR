TASK 1: ADVERSE DRUG REACTION (ADR) IDENTIFICATION BASIC

1.	PATIENT CASE PRESENTATION:
PATIENT PROFILE: 45-Year-old Female
PRESENTING SYMPTOMS: Generalized rash, severe itching, nausea, and dizziness
MEDICATION HISTORY: 
*Amoxicillin (started 5 days ago, scheduled course)
*Paracetamol (taken on as-needed basis)

2.CLINICAL CASUALITY & TIMELINE ANALYSIS:
TEMPORAL RELATIONSHIP:The patient’s symptoms began 5 days after starting amoxicillin. In clinical practice , beta-lactam-induced cutaneous hypersentivity reaction (like maculopapular rash and pruritus ) typically manifest between 4 to 10 days after starting therapy .  This perfectly aligns with the timelines.
DE-CHALLENGE CONSIDERATIONS: Paracetamol is intermittently used and carries a statistically lower correlation with sudden generalized pruritic rashes , making it an unlikely primary suspect compared to the continuos  5days course of an antibiotics.

3.FINAL ASSESSMENT & CONCLUSION:
Is the reaction drug – related?  YES
Suspected Drug: AMOXICILLIN
Type of Reaction:  Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) – Type IV (Delayed) Cutaneous Hypersensitivity Reaction.
Recommended Action: Under supervision, immediately discontinues Amoxicillin, initiates symptomatic relief for the allergic reaction (eg ., antihistamines) and switches the patient to a non-beta-lactam antibiotic alternative if therapy is still required.
